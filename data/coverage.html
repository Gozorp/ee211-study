4. Limitations, unverified items and source uncertainties

4.1 Video access and playback

  • No downloads. The videos are on YouTube, which offers no authorised download for them, so every lesson uses the official embedded YouTube player plus a direct link. You need an internet connection to watch.
  • Must be served, not opened as a file. YouTube refuses to play embeds on file:// pages (“Error 153”). Use Start-Study-Site.bat (http://localhost:8211). Clickable timestamps, live transcript highlighting and the remembered playback position all rely on the YouTube player API and work on that address. Playback was tested on localhost; embedding is allowed for all 13 videos.
  • YouTube may show ads before or during playback; that is controlled by YouTube.

4.2 Transcripts

  • None of the videos has human-made captions. The transcripts are YouTube’s automatic speech-recognition captions, regrouped into 15–25 s blocks. They were not checked word-by-word against the audio, and no separate audio transcription was made (that would require downloading the audio, which YouTube does not authorise).
  • During review, each video’s reviewer listed mis-heard technical terms (e.g. “why network” → wye network, “our a” → R_A, “ferret” → farad, “Henry’s” → henries). About 370 such fixes were applied; each fixed word is dotted-underlined and shows the original caption on hover. Grammar, filler words and non-technical mis-hearings were left as captioned. Some spoken numbers in the captions are wrong (examples below); the study guides use the values written on the instructor’s paper.
  • Caption timestamps are YouTube’s and are accurate to about a second; transcript blocks start at the first caption in the block.

4.3 How the videos were reviewed

  • Every in-topic part of every video was reviewed from the full transcript plus 16–54 captured frames per video of the instructor’s handwritten pages and the oscilloscope/LTspice screens, taken at each page change and every 45–90 s. Content that appeared only briefly between captured frames could have been missed. Items scrolled past too fast to read are listed below.
  • Out-of-topic parts of partially relevant videos (Wheatstone bridge, transformer theory, rectifier theory, phasors) were reviewed from the transcript only and are outlined, not taught, in each guide’s “Other content in this video” section.
  • Every worked example was recomputed in Python. Instructor slips are kept in the narrative and flagged in an amber “Source uncertainty” box with the corrected value.
  • Explanations, derivations and examples that the videos do not contain (e.g. Δ–Y derivation and balanced case, R_TH with dependent sources, V_OC/I_SC, solve-for-time with ln, discharge equations, reversed-polarity Millman branches) are added in purple “Additional teaching (not from the video)” boxes.
