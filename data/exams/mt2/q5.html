The two labels on the screen are scale settings: “5v” means each vertical division is 5 V, and “2ms” means each horizontal division is 2 ms. Every reading is (number of divisions) × (scale).

For the period, pick two points that look the same one cycle apart, for example a crest and the next crest, and count the horizontal divisions between them. Check with the two troughs.

For the voltages, count vertically. Peak: from the 0 V centre line (the marker on the left edge) up to a crest. Peak-to-peak: from the bottom of a trough to the top of a crest.

Unit conversions: 1 ms = 1000 μs, and \(f = 1/T\). If \(T\) is in ms, \(1/T\) comes out in kHz; multiply by 1000 for Hz.

  1. This is an oscilloscope reading question. The screen is a graph of voltage (up) against time (across), drawn over a grid of equal divisions. The two printed settings tell you what one division is worth:

    • Vertical scale “5v”: 5 V per division (V/div).
    • Time base “2ms”: 2 ms per division (time/div).

    The screen is 10 divisions across and 8 divisions tall. The centre horizontal line is 0 V; the filled marker on the left edge sits on it, so 0 V is the centre line and every voltage is measured from there. (Step 7 checks that the wave is centred on it.)

  2. Because every division is the same size, a distance on the screen measured in divisions is turned into a real quantity by multiplying by the setting for that axis. Times come from horizontal distances (time base); voltages come from vertical distances (V/div). The two readings are independent: the time base has nothing to do with the voltages, and the V/div setting has nothing to do with the period.

    The six parts ask for only three things, each in two forms: the period \(T\) (in ms and μs), the frequency \(f\) (in kHz and Hz), and the amplitude (peak and peak-to-peak).

  3. \[ T = n_T \times \text{(time/div)} \qquad f = \frac{1}{T} \]
    \[ V_{pk} = n_{pk} \times \text{(V/div)} \qquad V_{pp} = n_{pp} \times \text{(V/div)} \]
    • \(n_T\): horizontal divisions for one full cycle (between two equivalent points, such as crest to next crest).
    • \(T\): period, the time for one cycle, in seconds (s), milliseconds (ms) or microseconds (μs).
    • \(f\): frequency, cycles per second, in hertz (Hz) or kilohertz (kHz).
    • \(n_{pk}\): vertical divisions from the 0 V centre line to a crest. \(n_{pp}\): vertical divisions from a trough to a crest.
    • \(V_{pk}\): peak voltage, the highest value reached. \(V_{pp}\): peak-to-peak voltage, highest minus lowest. For a sine centred on 0 V, \(V_{pp} = 2V_{pk}\).

    Unit facts: \(1\ \text{ms} = 10^{-3}\ \text{s} = 1000\ \mu\text{s}\) and \(1\ \text{kHz} = 1000\ \text{Hz}\). Because \(1/10^{-3} = 10^{3}\), dividing 1 by a time in ms gives a frequency in kHz.

  4. Pick a feature that repeats once per cycle and find it twice:

    • The first crest is about a quarter of a division past the vertical line 2 divisions from the left edge (≈ 2¼ div). The next crest is about a quarter past the line at 7 divisions (≈ 7¼ div). Distance: \(7\tfrac14 - 2\tfrac14 = 5\) div.
    • Check with the troughs: ≈ 4¾ div and ≈ 9¾ div. Distance: 5 div again.
    \[ n_T = 5\ \text{div} \]

    Two different pairs of points give the same count, so you are measuring a whole cycle and not half of one (crest to trough is only 2½ div). Source note: a pixel measurement of the photographed exam gives 4.98 div between crests. The trace is a printed drawing, so readings carry about ±0.1 div of uncertainty, and the intended reading is the round 5 div.

  5. \[ T = n_T \times \text{(time/div)} = 5\ \text{div} \times 2\ \frac{\text{ms}}{\text{div}} = 10\ \text{ms} \]

    The “div” units cancel, leaving ms. a) \(T = 10\) ms.

    Convert to microseconds with \(1\ \text{ms} = 1000\ \mu\text{s}\):

    \[ T = 10\ \text{ms} \times 1000\ \frac{\mu\text{s}}{\text{ms}} = 10\,000\ \mu\text{s} \]

    b) \(T = 10\,000\ \mu\)s. In seconds this is \(T = 10 \times 10^{-3}\ \text{s} = 0.01\ \text{s}\), which is handy for the next step.

  6. Frequency is the number of cycles per second, the reciprocal of the period:

    \[ f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{10\ \text{ms}} = 0.1\ \frac{1}{\text{ms}} = 0.1\ \text{kHz} \]

    One over milliseconds is kilohertz, because \(1/10^{-3}\ \text{s} = 10^{3}\ \text{Hz}\). c) \(f = 0.1\) kHz.

    \[ f = 0.1\ \text{kHz} \times 1000\ \frac{\text{Hz}}{\text{kHz}} = 100\ \text{Hz} \]

    Same result straight from seconds: \(f = 1/0.01\ \text{s} = 100\ \text{Hz}\). d) \(f = 100\) Hz.

  7. Measure from the 0 V centre line, the line the left-edge marker points to. Positive voltages are above it and negative below it.

    • Crests: about halfway between the grid lines 2 and 3 divisions above the centre line, so \(n_{pk} \approx 2\tfrac12\) div above 0 V.
    • Troughs: about halfway between the lines 2 and 3 divisions below the centre line, ≈ 2½ div below 0 V.
    • Trough to crest: \(n_{pp} = 2\tfrac12 + 2\tfrac12 = 5\) div.

    The wave swings equally above and below 0 V, so it is centred on 0 V and the peak is half the peak-to-peak: \(n_{pk} = 5/2 = 2.5\) div.

    Source note: the pixel measurement gives crests at +2.43 div, a trough at −2.64 div and 5.07 div trough to crest. The 0.2 div difference between the crest and the trough heights is drawing and photo error. The peak-to-peak count is the most reliable reading, and it rounds to 5 div. That is why the peak is taken as half of it.

  8. \[ V_{pk} = n_{pk} \times \text{(V/div)} = 2.5\ \text{div} \times 5\ \frac{\text{V}}{\text{div}} = 12.5\ \text{V} \]

    e) \(V = 12.5\ \text{V}_{pk}\).

    \[ V_{pp} = n_{pp} \times \text{(V/div)} = 5\ \text{div} \times 5\ \frac{\text{V}}{\text{div}} = 25\ \text{V} \]

    f) \(V = 25\ \text{V}_{pp}\) (peak-to-peak, written “Vpkpk” on the exam). Check: \(V_{pp} = 2V_{pk} = 2 \times 12.5 = 25\) V.

    The wave reaches +12.5 V at each crest and −12.5 V at each trough, measured against the 0 V centre line.

  • Using the whole screen as the period. 10 div × 2 ms = 20 ms is the screen width, which holds two cycles here.
  • Mixing up the two scales. Horizontal divisions × time/div gives time; vertical divisions × V/div gives volts. 5 div × 5 = 25 “ms” is wrong.
  • Forgetting to multiply by the scale. 2.5 divisions is not 2.5 V; at 5 V/div it is 12.5 V.
  • Swapping peak and peak-to-peak. Peak is measured from the 0 V line to a crest; peak-to-peak from a trough to a crest. Here 12.5 Vpk = 25 Vpp.
  • Giving the RMS value. \(0.707 \times 12.5 = 8.84\) V is the RMS value. The question asks for peak and peak-to-peak, which you read directly from the screen.
  • Prefix slips in the conversions. 10 ms is 10 000 μs, not 10 μs. \(1/(10\ \text{ms})\) is 0.1 kHz, not 0.1 Hz. Write the unit at every step.
  • Reporting \(\omega\) as the frequency. \(2\pi f = 628.3\) rad/s is the angular frequency; \(f\) is 100 Hz.
  • Measuring from the bottom edge of the screen. Voltage is measured from the 0 V line marked on the left edge, not from the bottom of the grid.

Approach for a similar problem. Write down the two settings first, then make three counts: divisions per cycle, divisions from 0 V to a crest, divisions from trough to crest.

  • Different settings. Only the multipliers change. For example, at 2 V/div and 5 ms/div, a cycle 3⅓ div long is 16.7 ms (60 Hz), and a crest 2.5 div above centre is 5 V peak.
  • Readings between grid lines. Use the small tick marks on the centre axes (5 per division, 0.2 div each) if the screen has them; the exam screen does not, so estimate tenths of a division by eye, to about 0.1 div. For the period, measuring across several cycles and dividing by the number of cycles is more precise.
  • The 0 V marker is not on the centre line, or the wave is shifted up or down (a DC offset). Peak-to-peak is still trough to crest. The amplitude is half of the peak-to-peak, measured from the middle of the swing, and the DC level is the middle of the swing relative to the 0 V marker.
  • Other shapes (square, triangle). Period, peak and peak-to-peak are read the same way. Only RMS changes: \(0.707\,V_{pk}\) holds for sines only.
  • Asked for RMS or \(\omega\). For a sine, \(V_{RMS} = V_{pk}/\sqrt2\) (8.84 V here), and \(\omega = 2\pi f\) (628.3 rad/s here).
  • Working backwards (given \(f\), find the divisions). \(T = 1/f\), then divisions per cycle \(= T/\text{(time/div)}\). Divisions for the peak \(= V_{pk}/\text{(V/div)}\); it must be 4 or less to fit on an 8-division screen.
  • Unit pairs. \(1/\text{s} \to \text{Hz}\), \(1/\text{ms} \to \text{kHz}\), \(1/\mu\text{s} \to \text{MHz}\). Moving between ms and μs, or between kHz and Hz, shifts the decimal point three places.

How the concepts combine: the question joins two separate readings, a time reading on the horizontal axis (period, then frequency through \(f = 1/T\)) and a voltage reading on the vertical axis (peak, then peak-to-peak). Each is a division count times its own scale. The unit conversions (ms ↔ μs, kHz ↔ Hz) and the peak versus peak-to-peak distinction are where most points are lost.
