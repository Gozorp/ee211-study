Approach for a similar problem.

  1. Find the starting and final capacitor voltages. Uncharged start: \(v_C(0) = 0\). Final: the source voltage (the capacitor ends up acting like an open circuit).
  2. Find \(\tau = RC\), with \(R\) the resistance in series with the capacitor in the switch position you are analysing. Convert prefixes (μF, kΩ) to base units first.
  3. Convert every asked time to \(t/\tau\). Whole numbers: the chart value is exact (63.2 %, 86.5 %, 95 %, 98.2 %, 99.3 %). Otherwise use the equations.
  4. Get \(v_C\), then \(v_R = V - v_C\) by KVL, then \(i = v_R/R\). The same current flows through every series part.
  5. Discharging (a charged capacitor connected across a resistor, no source): \(v_C = V_0\,\mathrm{e}^{-t/\tau}\), the current has the same shape but flows the other way, and \(v_R\) has the size of \(v_C\).
  6. Asked for a time instead (e.g. "when does \(v_C\) reach 4 V?"): solve \(t = -\tau\ln(1 - v_C/V)\).
  7. The inductor version is Question 3 on this exam: same chart, but \(\tau = L/R\), and the inductor's current builds up while its voltage decays.
